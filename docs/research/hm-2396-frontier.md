## Recent results

- Schneider's August witness closes `2060 = 4·5·103` via the Cooper–Wallis
  construction. The repository's Python and Rust verifiers each checked all
  `2,120,770` distinct row pairs of the published matrix. See the
  [verification and historical search record](hm-2060-frontier.md).
- Cooley's October construction closes `2092 = 4(522+1)` using a cyclic
  bordered quadruple. Our Python and Rust verifiers each checked all
  `2,187,186` distinct row pairs of the published normalized matrix. The
  Wallis–Whiteman builder also produced a valid matrix from Cooley's four
  sequences. The compact seed has
  row sums `(2,0,0,0)`, combined periodic autocorrelation `-4` on all `521`
  nonzero shifts, and a common multiplier group of order `7`, generated by
  `181`, with `90` position orbits. The companion workspace had focused on
  two different subgroups, `<53>` and `<91>`. Kumar also published an
  order-`2092` witness using the unbordered order-`9` multiplier family over
  `Z_523` that the companion workspace had tried. [Cooley's construction
  register](https://github.com/Middletownbooks/Hadamard#readme) · [Kumar's
  certificates](https://github.com/vatskishan03/Hadamard/tree/d885022/hadamard/2092).
- Egan, Kotsireas, London and Ó Catháin independently verified and described
  the twelve August matrices and give `2396 = 4·599` as the smallest open order
  in Table 5 of their November revision. Kumar's November construction register
  records further orders and gives the same smallest gap. The general
  Hadamard conjecture remains open. [Egan et al., v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.27175v2#S4) ·
  [Kumar's November report](https://generalised.si/research/hadamard-matrices) ·
  [Kumar's frontier table](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/vatskishan03/Hadamard/d885022/FRONTIER.md).

  least `9` for length-`333` Legendre pairs. This bears on the original search

## Exact multiplier screens for 2396

Run `python3 py/screen_hm2396.py` from the repository root.

### Cyclic Goethals–Seidel at length 599

The prime `599` has a cyclic unit group of order `598 = 2·13·23`. A common
multiplier subgroup of order `d` fixes `0` and has `598/d` nonzero orbits of
size `d`. Thus each row sum is congruent to `598 ± 1` modulo `2d`.
The squared row sums must add to `2396`. Among the 28 sorted positive odd
quadruples, all survive for `d=2` and none survive for `d` in
`{13,23,26,46,299,598}`. Independent row negations allow positive row sums
without affecting complementarity or multiplier invariance.

### Wallis–Whiteman bordered array at length 598

The group `Z_598` is isomorphic to `Z_2 × Z_13 × Z_23`, and its unit group is
`C_12 × C_22`. A normalized cyclic `H_4^*` seed has row sums `(2,0,0,0)` and
combined periodic autocorrelation `-4` away from zero. Evaluating the combined
autocorrelation at the character `x ↦ (-1)^x` gives

\[
\sum_{i=1}^4 \left(\sum_{x=0}^{597}(-1)^x s_i(x)\right)^2 = 4(598+1) = 2396.
\]

The even and odd positions have identical multiplier orbit sizes because
translation by `299` commutes with every unit. Enumerating signed sums of the
even-position orbit sizes and imposing the row sums and the character identity
excludes `12` of the `32` subgroups.

The smallest surviving family, generated by `25` and `31`, has `24` position
orbits, `15` nonzero shift classes and one possible alternating-sum signature
`(36,10,18,26)` up to signs and permutations of the last three rows. Translating
each row independently by `299` makes its alternating sum nonnegative while
preserving orbit invariance and autocorrelation. The exact search enumerated all
four row pools, retaining one row per autocorrelation profile. The pool sizes
were `(1692,3402,110,252)`. There were `177,174` distinct sums from the first
and third pools and `857,304` pairs from the second and fourth pools. No sums
matched the required complement. A second calculation used the companion
workspace's orbit-pair incidence model and unpacked integer autocorrelation
tuples. It reproduced the pool sizes and the empty join. This excludes the
whole `<25,31>` family.

### Mod-11 restrictions on the fixed positions

The order-`11` subgroup fixes the `26` positions that are multiples of `23`,
and all other positions lie in orbits of size `11`. At a shift that is a
multiple of `23`, each term in the periodic autocorrelation is constant on
its order-`11` orbit. Modulo `11`, the full autocorrelation is therefore
equal to the autocorrelation of the row restricted to `Z_26`. Its combined
value at each nonzero shift must be `-4` modulo `11`.

For each alternating-sum signature, the script enumerates fixed-position
sign patterns whose even and odd sums can extend to the required full sums.
It then joins their modular autocorrelation profiles. The result excludes
all four signatures for `<3>` (30 position orbits) and all fourteen for
`<25>` (42 position orbits).

### Remaining bordered search

The smallest subgroup family still open in our screen is the order-`24`
subgroup `<45,47>`, with `48` position orbits and `47` nonzero shift classes.
The analogous mod-3 restriction uses the `46` positions that are multiples
of `13`. An exploratory run that materialized whole profile pools terminated
before deciding any case. It needs a bounded join or tighter filters.

A sign row can also compress by summing over each quotient fiber. If the fiber size is `k`,
the compressed quadruple's combined autocorrelation must be `-4k` for all
nonzero quotient shifts and `2396-4k` at zero. This follows by summing the
original combined autocorrelation over the shifts above each quotient shift.
Cooley's published length-`522` seed passed this identity at quotient lengths
`2`, `6`, `9`, `18`, `29`, and `58`. Kumar's broader November study also
uses quotient tests and estimates high search costs for the `2396` families
it screened. [Kumar's methods and frontier table](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/vatskishan03/Hadamard/d885022/FRONTIER.md).
